# Diary — 2026-10-22 (09)

## 0054: slow launches begin on efficiency cores — measured, replicated, cause not yet isolated

Worktree `ask-startup2`, from `a6a77e1`. Direct commands only. **Runtime sources are unchanged**: every overlay was
applied in this disposable tree and reverted, and the shipping binary under test was built from unmodified sources
(11,069,600 bytes, copied before any overlay build so a later build could not replace it).

## Re-measure on the latest main

The other worktrees had finished and landed (thinking levels, bounded prompt), so the earlier numbers described an
older tree. A fresh 40-pair same-artifact cohort (350 ms idle versus burst; load 5.7, memory pressure 1; 40/40
complete): paced cold p50 **13.32 ms** [12.81–15.20], p95 19.79, max 43.17; burst **11.97** [11.55–12.82]. Within budget
here. The earlier failing cohorts were therefore not a permanent property of the build.

## Observational campaign: what moves a cohort median

Forty cohorts of twenty paced launches (800 launches, 0 failed), each bracketed by `vm_stat`, load and the pressure
reading; nothing was induced. Cohort medians ran **12.28–25.24 ms** (median 13.63); one cohort exceeded 25 ms, 53 of 800
launches (6.6%) did. Pressure stayed at level 1 throughout, so this campaign did **not** sample the warning level.

| Cohort cold p50 vs … | Spearman rho (n=40; about 0.31 for p<0.05) |
|---|---|
| lifetime CPU time of the same launches | **+0.95** |
| mean host load | -0.09 |
| page-ins during the cohort | -0.15 |
| decompressions / compressions | +0.17 / +0.30 |
| free pages before | -0.44 |
| time order | +0.05 |

Slow cohorts are slow because the process spends **more CPU time**, not because it waits; the whole distribution shifts
(fastest ten cohorts p10/p50/p90 12.00/12.98/14.55 ms, slowest ten 13.07/19.82/25.92). Major faults per launch stayed
at 18 even in the slowest cohorts, so paging the binary in is not the cause. Free pages is weakly significant and is
reported, not explained.

## The starting core

A regenerated minimal overlay (`development/research/startup/core.patch`; the earlier `phases.patch` no longer applies after
the thinking-level work) records `pthread_cpu_number_np` at ArgumentParser's configuration. Logical CPUs 0-3 are the
efficiency cores on this host (checked in the IORegistry), 4-6 and 7-9 the two performance clusters (the 3+3 split is an
assumption about logical numbering; the efficiency boundary is the one that matters here).

Five hundred interleaved paced and burst launches of the traced binary (0 excluded):

| Starting core | Paced cold p50 [IQR] | Burst cold p50 [IQR] | lifetime CPU p50 |
|---|---|---|---|
| efficiency (0-3) | **23.59** [18.75–32.10], n=24 | **24.93** [22.02–27.41], n=19 | 16.7 / 18.7 ms |
| performance 4-6 | 13.67 [12.91–14.64], n=124 | 12.52 [12.04–13.17], n=108 | 11.2 / 10.4 ms |
| performance 7-9 | 13.68 [13.20–14.52], n=102 | 12.61 [12.08–13.29], n=123 | 11.2 / 10.6 ms |

Efficiency-core starts cost about **+10-12 ms wall and +6-8 ms CPU** in both cadences. The two performance clusters are
indistinguishable, so the earlier idea that a particular performance cluster explained the paced/burst difference is
**refuted**. Cadence by itself was worth about 1 ms (13.78 versus 12.70 ms), partly because 10% versus 8% of paced and
burst launches started on an efficiency core.

Across the 25 cohorts of that run the efficiency-core **share** predicted the cohort median: Spearman **+0.93**; a cohort
with 95% efficiency-core starts had p50 25.0 ms, cohorts with none 12.3-13.7 ms.

A second, independent run of 300 launches (five QoS arms pooled, 48% efficiency-core starts) reproduced the gap:
efficiency **24.23** [20.70–27.45] ms (CPU 17.45), performance 4-6 **15.53**, 7-9 **16.32** ms (CPU about 12.3-12.6). The
pooled median was 21.07 ms, over budget purely from the share of efficiency starts.

## What this corrects in diary 05

Diary 05 recorded "slow starts ran on efficiency cores" as **refuted as a universal explanation**, from one paced cohort
whose sampled cores were all performance cores yet still took about 19 ms. That cohort exists and is unexplained: a
performance-core start was also slow there, under a host load of about 21. The honest reading now is that efficiency-core
placement is a **strong, replicated factor but not the only one**. "Universal" was the word that failed, not the data.

## A manipulation check that failed

To test whether the launcher's QoS controls placement, a child set its thread QoS (interactive, initiated, default,
utility, background) after fork and before exec, 60 rotated rounds, 300 launches. The main thread reported QoS **21 in every
arm**, so the intervention did not reach the new process, and the efficiency-core share stayed 43-53% in all arms with
no dose-response. This says nothing about QoS as a lever: the experiment did not apply it. **The causal direction is
therefore untested.** What is established is association with large, replicated effect size; the starting core is
sampled at the first marker, after about 6-17 ms of CPU, so it is where the process was then, not proof of where it began.

## Consequences and what is still open

- The 25 ms median budget can fail on a loaded host because the scheduler placed many launches on efficiency cores,
  without any change in ask's code. The budget script cannot currently tell that apart from a regression.
- `Scripts/measure-startup.py` now reports cold-frame statistics **by starting core** when the overlay markers are
  present, counting what it left out, so a failing cohort can show its efficiency-core share. The efficiency boundary
  comes from `hw.perflevel1.logicalcpu` and assumes logical CPUs 0..n-1 are the efficiency cores (verified once, by hand).
  Three behavioural mutants of the new classification and leave-out accounting are each caught.
- Not decided here, and the owner's call (ADR-0038 set these budgets): whether budget verification should require a
  low efficiency-core share, report it beside the median, or stay as is. No limit changed.
- Real levers on ask's side are less startup CPU, since the penalty scales with CPU. Measured shares: about 6-17 ms of
  CPU precede the CLI marker, ArgumentParser about 1.8 ms, policy construction about 0.8 ms. Not attempted here.
- Untested: whether a process can influence its own placement (it starts before it can ask), a real QoS intervention
  such as `taskpolicy`, launches from a terminal versus this harness's parent, and the memory-pressure warning level.

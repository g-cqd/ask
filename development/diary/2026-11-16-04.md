## A session ended, then spoken on

CI's debug tests once saw `HTTPMCPSessionTests`' whole conversation end with a `POST` where the session's `DELETE` was
expected. Its log was not found again (four failed runs read, none holds it), and the conversation run 4,000 times at once
here, in release, ended with the `DELETE` every time. So the cause was sought by reading, then made to happen.

| Claim | Probe | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A message whose task had not reached the wire when the conversation closed is sent after the session's `DELETE` | `HTTPMCPCloseOrderTests`: a sign-in that keeps the message's header waiting, the conversation closed, the header let go | **Holds**: `["POST", "DELETE", "POST"]`. `close` cancels the tasks and does not wait for them, and `exchange` asked nobody before sending |
| With the conversation asked first, it is not | The same test, after | **Holds**: `["POST", "DELETE"]`; the target's 111 tests green under `LIBDISPATCH_COOPERATIVE_POOL_STRICT=1` |

Changed: `exchange` reads `closed` after its headers and before its `POST`, as `listen` already did before its `GET`. In the
flaky test the late message would be `notifications/initialized`, which nothing waits for: believed, not shown, the log being
lost.

Left: a message that passed the check and is overtaken by the whole of `close` before it sends, which needs `close` to wait
for the tasks it cancels, and so a transport that honours cancellation; the second `POST`, after a renewed sign-in, asks
nobody either.

## The screen's tests on a pool of one thread

`AskTUITests` never ended under `LIBDISPATCH_COOPERATIVE_POOL_STRICT=1`, which on this Mac is a cooperative pool of one
thread: 1177 tests started, 2 ended in four minutes. `sample` on the stalled process says who holds the thread.

| Claim | Probe | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A child process held for its life is what holds the pool (`CommandCheck.run`, as noted on 2026-11-15) | `/usr/bin/sample` of the stalled test process, three times | **Refuted** for this suite: the thread was in a test each time |
| `TerminalColoursTests` waits on a semaphore for a write a pool task makes | First sample: `OS_dispatch_semaphore.wait(wallTimeout:)` under the test, `PageFollower.ask` being `Task.detached` | **Holds**: 60 s lost, then a failure; awaited instead, the suite goes on |
| `RenderThreadScreenTests` waits on a semaphore for the screen it started as a task | Second sample: `semaphore_wait` under the test | **Holds**: for ever; awaited instead, 1112 of 1177 end |
| What stalls then is not a test | Third sample: the thread is in `PageFollower.ask`, in `SerializedConnection.write`, behind the lock the stuck first frame holds | **Holds**: TerminalKit writes the colour query on a pool thread, and a write waits for the one before it |

Changed: the two tests await what they blocked on. Both suites green three times on the usual pool.

Not changed, a follow-up for TerminalKit: `PageFollower.ask` makes a blocking terminal write from a pool task, so a terminal
stuck in a write holds a pool thread for as long. With the usual pool that is one thread of many; with one, a prompt
submitted meanwhile does not reach the conversation, which is what `RenderThreadScreenTests` then shows. So `AskTUITests`
still does not end under the strict pool, and other targets' suites are what that check is for until TerminalKit writes
from a thread of its own.

# 2026-11-15 (15): a client that goes away

Roadmap 0234 step 2, what the diary of 2026-11-15 (08) left as not handled: "a client that goes away while ask writes (ask dies
of the pipe, its jobs unstopped)", and a turn that runs to its end for nobody when the input ends.

| Claim | Probe | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Writing to a client that no longer reads ends ask | a pipe with its reading end closed, written through `DescriptorSink` | **Holds** before: the test process died, signal 13. After: the write throws `EPIPE` (`F_SETNOSIGPIPE` on the descriptor, as `CommandCheck` does for a program's input) |
| When the input ends, a turn goes on to its end | a directed conversation, the input ended mid-turn | **Holds** before: the run was never stopped, and the server waited for it. After: the run is cancelled at once, an approval still asked is abandoned, and the server finishes |
| The whole, through the real process | `probe-serve.py`, part G: a `bash` call of `sleep 48.6` running, then both of the client's ends closed | **Holds** after, three runs: ask ends with 0 and no `sleep` is left. Before: still running 20 s later |
| The tests can fail | the cancellation at the input's end made a no-op | **Holds**: both turn tests red, by their time limit |

How: the bytes a server reads are wrapped (`ChunksUntilGone`), and their end cancels every session's turn exactly as the
client's own `session/cancel` would. The connection then has its answers at once, `run` returns, and `ask serve` stops what
the sessions left running (jobs, MCP servers), as it did when a client left between turns.

Not handled: a client that stops reading and keeps its input open. Its turn's updates fail to be written and are dropped; the
turn itself goes on until the client cancels or leaves.

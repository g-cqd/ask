### The performance trap (ADR-0016)

The benchmark `loop/one turn after 500 turns` went from 211 to 298 µs, +41%. The cause was the check
building the whole active branch to find the newest response. A walk up from the leaf, which stops at the
first response, brought it to 247 µs.

The rest would not reproduce. A tree copy was blamed, and moving the read under the lock changed nothing.
With load averages of 4 to 8 from another session's compile, cross-build numbers varied by ±15%, and
differences in code layout alone can move them.

An interleaved A/B in one process, check on against off over 40 rounds, measured +0.9, +3.5 and +7 µs on
about 345 µs: under 2%. The check was then made synchronous, so a turn that does not compact pays no
`async` call. The final cross-build pairs at 500 turns:

| Build | p50 (µs) |
|---|---|
| main | 208, 240 |
| this branch | 272, 228 |

Those overlap within the noise. The lesson: on a shared machine, compare variants inside one process,
interleaved, before believing a cross-build delta.

### Mistakes

- **Probe E's first version** expected the summary to be the first text in the request. ask's own prompt
  always leads (ADR-0007), so the check was wrong, not the code.
- **A grep for `session`** matched the new notice ("The session file keeps them all"), and broke a dump
  command.
- **`sample` from Homebrew shadows `/usr/bin/sample`,** and the harness's process lived too briefly to
  sample usefully. In-process A/B worked better.

### Live

compaction summarised a summary.

Telling the summariser to leave out ask's standing instructions helped only partly: it still restates a
few lines.

### The user's idea, recorded

Roadmap 0032 records the user's idea: a summary kept up to date in the background by a cheap or local
model, and an advisor for when to compact. It is to be investigated, not built. The `clear_thinking` and
`clear_tool_uses` edits found today are its cheap tier.
